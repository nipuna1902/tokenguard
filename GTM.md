# GTM.md

TokenGuard’s ideal target user is not “startups” broadly. The most likely early adopter is a technical founder or engineering manager at a 5–40 person startup that has rapidly adopted AI tooling without centralized spend oversight. These companies typically operate in engineering-heavy environments where employees independently subscribe to products like Cursor, Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, and Gemini using company cards. Over time, AI spend becomes fragmented, redundant, and difficult to evaluate operationally.

The trigger moment for using TokenGuard is usually financial anxiety combined with operational uncertainty. Right before wanting this tool, users are likely searching things like:

- “best AI coding tools for startups”
- “Cursor vs Copilot pricing”
- “AI tooling cost optimization”
- “reduce OpenAI API spend”
- “how much should startups spend on AI tools”

They are also likely doom-scrolling engineering Twitter/X discussions around AI pricing increases, model releases, or “what AI stack are you using?” conversations.

The communities where these users actually spend time are highly specific:

- r/LocalLLaMA
- r/startups
- r/SaaS
- Indie Hackers
- Hacker News
- AI engineering Discord servers
- Founder Slack groups
- Build-in-public Twitter/X circles
- YC and startup engineering discussions

To get the first 100 users with a $0 paid budget, I would focus entirely on distribution-native content rather than generic “content marketing.” The first strategy would be posting intentionally opinionated audit screenshots on Twitter/X showing examples like “This 8-person startup is wasting $1,200/month on overlapping AI subscriptions.” Those posts naturally invite discussion and quote tweets.

Second, I would submit TokenGuard to Hacker News with a positioning angle around “AI tool sprawl inside startups.” Hacker News responds well to operational tooling and transparent pricing discussions.

Third, I would directly DM founders and indie hackers offering free manual audits in exchange for feedback and permission to anonymize screenshots. Those screenshots would become social proof assets.

The unfair distribution advantage comes from Credex itself. Because Credex already operates adjacent to infrastructure finance and startup spend optimization, TokenGuard can become both:

- a lead-generation mechanism,
- and a trust-building operational finance tool.

A normal indie founder would struggle to monetize optimization insights directly, but Credex already has downstream financial infrastructure relationships.

If this strategy works, realistic week-one traction would look like:

- 300–500 unique visitors,
- 40–60 completed audits,
- 10–15 saved reports,
- 3–5 consultation requests,
- and at least one founder sharing their audit publicly.
